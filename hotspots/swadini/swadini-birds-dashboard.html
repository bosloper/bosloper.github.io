Annual Overview — Species Richness & Records

Bird observations were made across seven survey seasons from May 2019 to 29 August 2025, as a secondary component of broader biodiversity surveys focused primarily on Odonata. The sparse pre-2025 record counts (27–76 per year) reflect that birds were recorded incidentally during other survey work rather than as a primary target. From 2025, the observer's use of the Observation.org mobile app enabled photo-free recording, greatly increasing the volume and consistency of bird records — 508 of the 806 total records (63%) come from 2025 alone.

The 2020 season was constrained to August–October by COVID-19 lockdown restrictions. The 2019 season included two visits: May–August and November. These coverage differences must be kept in mind when interpreting annual species counts.

Effort & Recording Context
The dramatic increase in 2025 records reflects a change in recording method rather than a genuine change in bird diversity. The 2025 count of 67 species is the most reliable single-season estimate; the 4 core species (present all 7 years) should be understood as a minimum — species consistently noticed even during low-effort seasons. As recording effort normalises in future years, the core count will increase substantially.

Section 8
Summary & Outlook

Seven seasons of bird observations at Swadini, Blyde River Canyon Nature Reserve, have documented 95 species across 42 families within a ~1.1 km² study area. The site's mixed habitat — savannah and thicket transitioning to indigenous riverine forest along the Blyde River — supports a characteristically broad assemblage spanning forest specialists (Narina Trogon, Half-collared Kingfisher, African Finfoot), open-country species (Verreaux's Eagle, African Harrier-Hawk), and a rich understorey community of bushshrikes, sunbirds, waxbills and barbets.

Recording effort was highly uneven across the survey period. From 2019 to 2024, birds were logged incidentally alongside primary Odonata surveys, producing 27–76 records per season. From 2025, a deliberate shift toward systematic weekly recording — combined with the Observation.org mobile app enabling photo-free submissions — yielded 508 records and 67 species in a single season, representing the most complete inventory of the site to date. The 4 core species recorded in all 7 seasons (Trumpeter Hornbill, Red-headed Weaver, Retz's Helmetshrike, Cape White-eye) should be understood as a floor, not a ceiling: they were noticed even during the lowest-effort seasons and form the nucleus of a much larger permanent community.

The seasonal rainfall record from the adjacent Blyderivier Botaniese Reservaat provides ecological context: the 2023/24 season was 15% below the long-term mean, with an unusually dry October–December 2023. While the direct impact on bird numbers is difficult to quantify from the existing data, the concurrent decline in butterfly and Odonata richness in 2024 is consistent with a site-wide drought response.

Weekly Sub-Projects — A Method for Improving Species Coverage

From 2025, the observer adopted a weekly sub-project approach: rather than recording opportunistically across the season, each IOC week is treated as a discrete unit with the explicit goal of maximising the number of species detected (not individual counts). This systematic weekly effort, combined with the mobile app, produced the dramatic increase in both records and species richness seen in 2025.

The method is particularly well-suited to this site: the compact ~1.1 km² area can be covered thoroughly in repeated passes within a single week, and the transition between savannah and riverine forest habitats means that brief, targeted surveys of each habitat type yield complementary species lists. Weekly sub-projects also create a consistent temporal framework that makes year-on-year comparisons more meaningful — directly comparable to the IOC-week analysis used throughout this dashboard for Odonata and butterflies.

As this method becomes established, the core species count is expected to grow substantially. The 2025 baseline of 67 species and 95 cumulative species will serve as the reference point against which future seasons are measured.

Notable Records

Pel's Fishing Owl (Scotopelia peli) — recorded in week 32, 2025. A large, localised owl of riparian forest; its presence confirms the quality of the riverine habitat along the Blyde River within the study area. Single-year record to date.

African Finfoot (Podica senegalensis) — recorded in 6 of 7 seasons along the Blyde River. A secretive, under-recorded species that is a reliable indicator of intact, undisturbed riverine forest. Its consistent presence at Swadini is ecologically significant.

Crowned Eagle (Stephanoaetus coronatus) — recorded in 3 seasons (2021–2023). Africa's most powerful eagle, requiring large territories and intact forest. Its occurrence at the site confirms that the canyon holds sufficient habitat and prey base to support this apex predator.

Narina Trogon (Apaloderma narina) — recorded in 5 seasons, confirming it as a regular component of the forest understorey community. One of the most sought-after forest birds in southern Africa.

Priorities for Future Seasons
  • · Continue weekly sub-projects throughout the survey window to build a robust multi-year presence/absence record
  • · Extend coverage into September–October where possible — currently the most under-sampled period
  • · Target confirmed but infrequently recorded species (African Wood Owl, Thick-billed Cuckoo, Scaly-throated Honeyguide) for repeated detection to confirm residency status
  • · Integrate rainfall and ERA5 temperature data with annual species richness to quantify climate–biodiversity relationships
  • · Maintain photographic evidence for species not yet documented with photos at this site
