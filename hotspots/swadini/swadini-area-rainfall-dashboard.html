Annual Rainfall Totals by Season
August–July rainfall seasons 2000/01 to 2024/25. Gold dashed line = long-term mean (578 mm). Red = below 450 mm (drought threshold). Blue = above 700 mm (wet season).
Key patterns: Rainfall is highly variable, ranging from 338 mm (2003/04) to 1,003 mm (2004/05) — nearly a 3× difference between extremes. Five seasons fell below 450 mm: 2001/02, 2003/04, 2005/06, 2006/07, and 2018/19. The 2023/24 season (492 mm) — coinciding with the low butterfly year — was below the long-term mean but not in the extreme drought category, suggesting that seasonal distribution rather than total may explain the observed butterfly scarcity.
Mean Monthly Rainfall Profile
Average rainfall per calendar month across all complete seasons (2000/01–2024/25). The rainy season runs November–April, peaking sharply in January.
Seasonal structure: 96% of annual rainfall falls in the October–April window. January alone contributes ~22% of the annual total on average. The dry season (May–September) is reliable — fewer than 37 mm on average across five months combined. This aligns closely with the butterfly survey window (approximately IOC weeks 17–35 = late April to early September), which spans the dry season and the transition into it.

Rainfall in Butterfly Survey Years (2019–2025)
Monthly rainfall for the seven butterfly survey seasons, shown as overlapping lines. The survey window (approx. IOC weeks 17–35, roughly late April to early September) is highlighted.
2023/24 in context: Total season rainfall was 492 mm — 15% below the long-term mean. More significantly, the October–December 2023 period was unusually dry (just 101 mm combined vs. a long-term mean of 224 mm — the driest Oct–Dec on record), potentially impacting larval food plants and adult nectar sources during the critical pre-survey buildup period. The subsequent butterfly season (IOC weeks 17–44, 2024) showed the lowest species count (58) and record count (196) of any high-effort year, consistent with a lag effect from the preceding dry season.

Note on 2020: The 2020 butterfly season ran mid-July to November (IOC weeks 33–42) due to COVID-19 lockdown, missing the typical May–June diversity peak entirely. The low species count (48) reflects survey timing rather than rainfall conditions.

Data Attribution & Acknowledgement

The rainfall record presented in this dashboard was compiled by Johan (Pine) Pienaar, a resident of a private residential estate situated on the western bank of the Blyde River, Limpopo Province — immediately adjacent to the Blyde River Canyon Nature Reserve across the provincial border in Mpumalanga. Mr Pienaar recorded daily precipitation at his personal station and summarised the data as monthly totals, maintaining this record with meticulous care from 1999 through to 2026 — a period of more than 25 years. The data and all intellectual property belong to Mr Johan (Pine) Pienaar in his personal capacity.

This dataset was kindly made available through the assistance of Eugene Fourie, a fellow resident of the same estate, who facilitated contact between the observer and Mr Pienaar in April 2026.

Long-term local rainfall records of this quality and continuity are rare and invaluable for ecological research. The Swadini butterfly survey analysis presented alongside this data would not have been possible without Mr Pienaar's dedication to careful, consistent observation over more than two decades.
